CHEO Research Institute · Healthcare professional education

Research Associate / Instructional Designer

2020–present

At CHEO Research Institute, I lead the design, development and evaluation of ADHD diagnostic-interview learning for healthcare professionals, including 48 actor-based video simulations. I work with clinicians, researchers, instructors, actors and technical partners to define the educational purpose, intended interview performance and evaluation questions; clinical experts retain authority for clinical content. I translate that expertise into explanation, structured practice, simulation, assessment and feedback across instructor-led, webinar and self-directed formats, while coordinating plans, review cycles, production, Brightspace implementation, quality assurance and delivery support. I use learner, instructor, technical and evaluation evidence to guide revision; this portfolio describes the research design rather than unpublished findings.

  • Clarify the clinical purpose, learner context and existing strengths
  • Define interview performance, outcomes and evaluation questions
  • Align explanation, practice, simulation, assessment and feedback
  • Coordinate production, Brightspace implementation, QA, evaluation and revision
01

Selected work example

Self-directed course architecture and searchable learning

The self-directed course combined a structured Brightspace pathway, chaptered video, searchable reference material and learner-controlled history content. The aim was not to replace live instruction with pages of information, but to let healthcare professionals locate, revisit and connect specialist explanation before applying it in interview decisions.

02

Selected work example

Interactive video, decisions and feedback

Interactive video paused realistic interviews at decision points. Learners chose how to proceed, reviewed the reasoning behind the options, retried where appropriate and connected their choices to interview dimensions and common errors. Two short excerpts show the interaction in motion; the storyboard below shows the wider observe–choose–feedback sequence.

Question choice and rating

An observed parent interview leads to a choice of probe, followed by explanatory feedback that connects the decision to interview technique.

Read clip transcript and visual sequence

The interface asks for examples of behaviour. The parent says, “That would be everything. I’d say it’s hard for Julia to keep track of most of her things.” A decision menu then asks how the interviewer should proceed, followed by written feedback.

Systematic reporter bias

An interview excerpt is followed by a learner decision and rationale, making a common information-gathering error visible and discussable.

Read clip transcript and visual sequence

The module introduces systematic reporter bias. The interviewer says, “I’d like to talk a little bit about Olivia’s ability to sustain her attention on tasks that require some mental effort.” After the exchange, a decision menu and written feedback connect the observation to a better next step.

03

Selected work example

Instructor-led training, simulation and evaluation

The facilitated programme centred on repeated interview practice with actors, structured observation and feedback. Working with clinicians, researchers and actors, I translated the interview into scenarios, performance guidance, rating criteria and debrief structures, then coordinated Brightspace implementation, delivery support and quality assurance. I also coordinated a completed mixed-methods non-inferiority study comparing the self-directed and instructor-led programmes. A manuscript is in preparation, so this portfolio shows the evaluation design rather than unpublished results.
