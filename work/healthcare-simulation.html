CHEO Research Institute · Healthcare professional education

ADHD diagnostic-interview training across live and self-directed formats

Working with clinicians and healthcare researchers, I translated a complex diagnostic-interview task into instructor-led, webinar and self-directed learning with interactive content, structured practice and simulation.

Role
Lead instructional design and development
Audience
Healthcare professionals
Scope
Live, webinar and self-directed learning in Brightspace
Principal outputs
48 simulations, interactive course, assessment and supporting resources

Visual story

From searchable explanation to interview decisions.

On smaller screens, swipe or scroll through the folio. With the folio focused, use the arrow keys.

The brief

Healthcare professionals needed to notice relevant evidence, apply diagnostic criteria consistently and make sound decisions across interviews that did not present in identical ways. The learning therefore had to support clinical judgement, not simple recall. Varied cases and explanatory feedback needed to make the underlying reasoning visible without reducing the task to a checklist.

The self-directed course also had to carry forward the explanation, guided practice and reasoning-focused feedback available in live training. Clinical experts retained authority for the content while I designed a coherent learning system that could be delivered, tested and evaluated in Brightspace, with consistent media, assessment and completion logic.

End-to-end contribution

What I designed and delivered

Analysed the interview task

I worked with clinicians and researchers to identify evidence, decisions, observable actions and recurring learner difficulties, drawing on experience delivering the live training.

Designed learning and simulation

I structured the live, webinar and online pathways, then developed storyboards, scripts, activities, assessment and varied actor-based cases with reasoning-focused feedback.

Implemented the course

I built and tested the Brightspace flow, media, captions, completion logic, learner controls, accessibility, navigation and supporting documentation.

Coordinated QA and evaluation

I brought clinical review, learner feedback, instructor input, technical testing and a formal non-inferiority study into one documented revision cycle.

What the work produced

Quality assurance covered accuracy, comprehension, accessibility, navigation, assessment, media and LMS behaviour. Research, learner feedback and technical findings informed revision. Course maintenance remained documented so later changes could be traced across content, activities, assessment and delivery. Because the study findings are not yet published, I report the evaluation process rather than results.
