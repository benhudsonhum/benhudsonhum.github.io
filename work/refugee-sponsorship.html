Refugee Hub / Global Refugee Sponsorship Initiative

Delivering blended refugee-sponsorship training in a six-month engagement

In a defined six-month engagement, I worked with an IRCC content specialist and UNHCR and IRCC stakeholders to turn detailed sponsorship requirements into modular online preparation, facilitator-led workshops and practical learner and facilitator resources.

The challenge

The engagement had a limited timeframe, specialist source content and a defined set of online and in-person deliverables. Detailed policy and process requirements had to become actions that sponsorship groups could understand, discuss and apply.

The package also needed enough consistency to support stakeholder review while allowing facilitators to interpret examples and adapt discussion to local contexts.

My role

I analysed the source content and requirements with an IRCC content specialist, identified essential learner actions, and designed the relationship between online preparation and facilitated application. I then developed scenarios, learner checks, graphics, learner materials and facilitator resources.

I designed the learning system with policy specialists and supported review by UNHCR and IRCC stakeholders before pilot delivery. Their subject and program expertise informed revision and finalization of the contracted package.

Mode relationship

Use each mode for the work it supports best.

Online preparation

Modular self-directed learning established stable background knowledge, process stages and essential checks before group work.

Facilitator-led application

Workshops created space for discussion, interpretation, scenarios, group planning and adaptation to the participants' context.

Key design decisions

Bring requirements back at the point of action.

1. Translate requirements into learner actions

Organize content around what sponsorship groups must prepare, decide, document and coordinate.

2. Use scenarios and checks

Ask learners to interpret realistic situations and apply guiding principles instead of only reviewing source content.

3. Build a consistent visual language

Use stable page structures, diagrams, colour cues and signposting across online and workshop materials.

4. Design for adaptation without losing the core

Separate stable sponsorship principles from details that require facilitated interpretation or local adaptation.

Review and revision

Content-specialist and stakeholder review checked accuracy, clarity and the relationship between online and facilitator-led materials. Scenarios, checks, visual cues and workshop resources were revised before pilot delivery.

By the end of the six-month engagement, the online resource, workshop curriculum and facilitator materials had been reviewed and prepared for pilot delivery.

What the work produced

  • Modular online learning for sponsorship preparation
  • Scenarios, applied checks and learner activities
  • Facilitator-led workshop curriculum
  • Learner and facilitator resources
  • Graphics and a consistent visual language
  • A stakeholder-reviewed blended package prepared for pilot delivery

What I learned

The design problem was not to make the policy disappear. It was to preserve what mattered while changing the form in which learners encountered it. The most useful structure began with the decisions and actions of a sponsorship group, then brought requirements back in at the point they were needed.
