Theatre for a Change · Malawi and Southern Africa

Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor

2014–2016 · Later freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer

At Theatre for a Change in Malawi, I helped develop and codify a participatory learning methodology, then applied it across curricula, manuals, facilitator resources, training programmes, assessment and revision. As Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor—and later as a freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer—I connected programme needs to knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes, participatory activity sequences and evidence of behaviour in practice. The work used embodied, spatial and scenario-based methods to surface experience, make relationships visible, support open reflection and rehearse alternative strategies. I also facilitated learning, developed facilitators and used observation, participant response and evaluation evidence to improve guidance, sequencing and resources. The achievement was not a single manual, but a coherent, adaptable method linking participation to intended learning and practical application.

  • Define knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes
  • Codify participatory methods and resources
  • Develop facilitators through practice and feedback
  • Revise guidance from evidence
01

Selected work example

Life Skills: companion to the national teacher-training curriculum

The Life Skills manual is a later, mature expression of a methodology developed over years. It organizes learning around knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes, moving from engagement and inquiry toward behavioural practice. I designed many other curricula and manuals, including Girls’ Education Challenge work, but feature this one because its progression and guidance make the methodology visible.

02

Selected work example

Codifying a participatory learning methodology

I helped codify an adaptable pattern: define knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes; surface experience and assumptions; make relationships visible; explore, observe and reflect; rehearse alternatives; and apply learning to situations. Delivery evidence informed guidance and sequencing. Sculpting, Mapping and Touch Tag served outcomes rather than fixed stages.

03

Selected work example

Facilitator development in Eswatini

Over three weeks, 20 youth peer educators experienced modelled activities, rehearsed participatory methods, led practice, observed peers and used feedback to improve delivery. The sequence transferred the methodology through practice and prepared participants to facilitate independently in their own programme context, rather than treating facilitator development as a one-off event.
