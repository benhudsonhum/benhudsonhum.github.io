Theatre for a Change · Malawi and Southern Africa

Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor

2014–2016 · Later freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer

At Theatre for a Change in Malawi, I served as Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor, later returning as a freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer. I analysed organizational and programme needs, connected them to curriculum architecture and participatory activity design, and produced facilitator and learner resources for varied delivery contexts. My role extended through facilitation, facilitator development, implementation support, assessment and evaluation, using delivery evidence to revise instructions, sequencing, visual resources and guidance. Work included the 90-activity Life Skills manual and a three-week Eswatini programme for 20 youth peer educators. This was practical curriculum work judged by what facilitators and learners could do in the room.

  • Analyse organizational and programme learning needs
  • Design curricula, activities and practical resources
  • Facilitate and prepare facilitators for delivery
  • Use assessment and implementation evidence to revise
01

Selected work example

Life Skills: companion to the national teacher-training curriculum

The 90-activity manual incorporated Theatre for a Change’s participatory methodology into the existing Life Skills curriculum. I designed active-learning structures rather than simply adding content topics, pairing clear outcomes with preparation, embodied activity, discussion, reflection and debriefing. Visual explanations and safeguarding guidance helped facilitators make complex or sensitive ideas usable in classrooms.

Selected work example

Curriculum architecture and facilitator support

I connected organizational and programme needs to outcomes, participatory sessions, facilitator guidance and assessment. Delivery evidence was part of the same system: observation, facilitator experience and evaluation identified where language, pacing, activity instructions or supporting resources needed revision before wider reuse across programmes and facilitator groups.

03

Selected work example

Facilitator development in Eswatini

I designed and delivered a three-week programme for 20 youth peer educators. Participants first experienced modelled activities, then moved through participatory rehearsal, participant-led practice, observation and feedback. The sequence prepared them to understand the method, test their own facilitation decisions and move toward independent delivery without relying on participant photography.
