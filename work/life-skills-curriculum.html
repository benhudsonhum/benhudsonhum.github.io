Theatre for a Change · Malawi and Southern Africa

Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor

2014–2016 · Later freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer

At Theatre for a Change in Malawi, I helped develop and codify a participatory learning methodology, then applied it across curricula, manuals, facilitator resources, training programmes and assessment. As Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor—and later as a freelance instructional designer and curriculum developer—I connected programme aims to knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes, sequenced participatory activities around those outcomes and defined evidence of behaviour in practice. The methods used movement, space, roleplay and scenarios to surface experience, make relationships visible and give learners room to test alternatives. I also facilitated learning, developed facilitators and revised guidance from observation, participant response and evaluation, helping establish a shared method that could be adapted across programmes without losing sight of the intended learning.

  • Define knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes
  • Turn participatory methods into usable guidance and resources
  • Develop facilitators through modelling, practice and feedback
  • Revise programmes and resources from delivery evidence

Selected work example

Life Skills: companion to the national teacher-training curriculum

The 90-activity Life Skills manual shows this approach in a form designed for teacher education. Developed for Malawi's Ministry of Education as a companion to the national teacher-education curriculum, it connects knowledge, attitude and skill outcomes with clear activity sequences, discussion, reflection and behavioural practice. I feature it here because the progression and facilitator guidance make the underlying methodology visible.

02

Selected work example

Team Girl Malawi learner workbook

The Team Girl Malawi workbook translated participatory sessions into a repeatable learner-facing format. Each spread combines a worked example, an activity and a reflection prompt, using illustrations and simple response formats to help adolescent learners revisit rights, health, communication, bullying and menstrual-health content beyond the facilitated session.

03

Selected work example

Codifying a participatory learning methodology

I helped turn TfaC's participatory practice into a teachable system at two scales. The Workshop Journey sequences knowledge, attitudinal exploration, skills practice and reflection within a session. The illustrated Mphatso journey shows how repeated participation, access to support, performance and public voice were intended to accumulate across a longer programme pathway.

04

Selected work example

Facilitator development, delivery and evaluation

Across TfaC assignments, I modelled participatory methods, supported rehearsal and participant-led practice, and used observation and assessment to improve delivery. In the 2018 VSO partnership in Eswatini (then Swaziland), the work included research and planning, 10 days of training with 20 participants, five days of supervised implementation and later comparative impact analysis across five pilot countries.
