Theatre for a Change · Malawi and Southern Africa

Life-skills curriculum and facilitator development

As Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor—and later as a freelance instructional designer—I connected organizational needs, participatory learning, facilitator development, implementation and evaluation across varied delivery settings.

Role
Curriculum, Training and Assessment Advisor; freelance instructional designer
Audience
Facilitators, peer educators and programme participants
Scope
Organizational curriculum and programme-specific delivery
Principal outputs
90-activity manual, resources, facilitator development and evaluation

Visual story

A curriculum made usable in the room.

On smaller screens, swipe or scroll through the folio. With the folio focused, use the arrow keys.

The brief

Theatre for a Change needed participatory life-skills learning that could remain coherent across programmes, facilitators and delivery settings. Activities alone were insufficient: the organization also needed curriculum structure, explicit outcomes, usable safeguarding guidance and a way to prepare facilitators for reflective practice.

I worked from organizational and programme needs through design, delivery and evaluation. The result had to support immediate facilitation while remaining adaptable enough for different audiences, partners and contexts. It also needed to help facilitators connect embodied activity, structured reflection and application rather than treating participation as an end in itself.

Curriculum to implementation

What I designed and delivered

Mapped needs to a curriculum

I connected organizational priorities and programme requirements to outcomes, themes, participatory sessions and assessment, creating a structure that could guide adaptation.

Developed practical resources

I produced the 90-activity Life Skills manual alongside learner materials, visual explanations and facilitator safeguards for dependable use in the room.

Prepared facilitators to deliver

I designed facilitator development and led a three-week Eswatini programme for 20 youth peer educators using modelling, rehearsal, observation and feedback.

Revised from implementation evidence

I used observation, facilitator experience, participant response and programme evaluation to improve sequencing, language, activity guidance and resource usability.

What the work produced

Delivery evidence was part of the design cycle. Facilitation, observation and evaluation identified where language, pacing, activity instructions or supporting resources needed revision before wider reuse across programmes and facilitator groups. That evidence helped keep the manual, supporting resources and facilitator preparation aligned while preserving enough flexibility for different programmes, delivery contexts and partner requirements.
